Peer-to-peer networking is a method of communication where each participant acts as both a client and a server simultaneously. Unlike traditional networks with a central hub, data is passed directly between individual computers without a dedicated backbone. This architecture allows users to share files, photos, and media files freely without the need for a central storage server. Because the system is decentralized, the network remains functional even if a specific machine fails or is turned off. Security is a primary concern in P2P networks, as the absence of a central authority makes it difficult to enforce strict access controls. Users must manage their own encryption keys and file permissions to ensure data remains private and secure during transmission. The speed of the network is often slower than centralized systems because the data must travel through multiple hops rather than a direct link. This model is particularly effective for large file sharing applications like BitTorrent, which allows users to download content by downloading small chunks from many sources. However, the lack of a central administrator can lead to issues with copyright enforcement and the potential for malicious behavior among participants. Despite these challenges, peer-to-peer networking continues to evolve as a popular choice for building resilient and flexible communication networks.
