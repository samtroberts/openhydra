Peer-to-peer networking is a method of connecting devices where each participant acts as both a client and a server, sharing resources directly without a central authority. Unlike traditional networks, there is no dedicated router, switch, or central server to manage traffic or distribute data. Instead, every device in the network, known as peers, can respond to requests as if it were the main network node. Users can share files, peripherals, and bandwidth with one another as long as they agree to the terms of the connection. This architecture relies on protocols like FTP, SMB, or BitTorrent to facilitate the transfer of data between connected computers. Because there is no central management, the network can be more flexible but also more prone to security vulnerabilities if not handled carefully. Users often get more control over their own devices as they can choose which resources to expose and how to respond to requests. However, as networks get larger, managing overlapping connections and preventing conflicts can become significantly more complex and difficult. Despite these challenges, peer-to-peer remains a popular choice for small groups, home networks, and specific applications like file sharing and media streaming.
